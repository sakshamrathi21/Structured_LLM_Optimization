\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
We evaluate \textcolor{purple}{Feather} on a variety of popular models, GPU configurations, and two datasets. Our evaluation seeks to answer the following questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How does our scheduler's performance scale across diverse workloads, varying numbers of prefix groups, decode tokens, and input sequence lengths?
    \item What performance improvement does \textcolor{purple}{Feather} achieve for both online and offline serving under real-world workloads?
    \item What is the contribution of each component of \textcolor{purple}{Feather} to the overall performance gain?
    \item How much throughput degradation is caused by the replacement of some components with the ideas presented in existing work?
    \item What is the impact of \textcolor{purple}{Feather}'s overhead?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Model and server configurations.} We use Qwen \cite{qwen} models with 2B parameters, LLama3 \cite{llama3modelcard} with 8B parameters, and LongChat \cite{longchat2023} with 13B parameters. For most of our experiments, we use the RTX 6000 Ada Generation GPU \cite{nvidia_rtx6000_datasheet} with 48 GB of HBM and a cache size of 96 MB for L2. The maximum batch size is set to $500$ for all these experiments. \textcolor{red}{present results on another GPU? A100 for PAT? Any other GPU needed too?}

\textbf{Workloads:} Similar to prior work, the arrival pattern of requests is generated by a Poisson process. The input lengths and output lengths of requests are sampled from the following real-world datasets.

L-Eval \cite{an2023leval}: It contains human-labeled query-response pairs on diverse tasks, such as summarization and question answering. The range of sequence length in this dataset is 2.7K - 210.5K tokens.

LongBench \cite{bai2024longbench2} encompasses a curated subsample of elongated data, ranging from 4K to 10K context lengths, excerpts from 21 datasets across 6 categories, including tasks such as multi-document question answering, summarization, and code completion.

We consider vLLM~\cite{kwon2023efficient} (FCFS), SGLang~\cite{zheng2024sglangefficientexecutionstructured} (all three policies - FCFS, LPM, DFS-W), and DynamicBatching~\cite{pang2025optimizing} as our baselines because they represent the state-of-the-art for LLM inference.

\textbf{Key Metrics.} Throughput (toks/s) measures how many output tokens the system can generate per second and indicates its overall serving capacity. Higher throughput indicates better utilization of GPU compute and memory resources. Time Between Tokens (TBT) is the average time gap between consecutive generated tokens during decoding. It reflects how smoothly and responsively the model produces output, which is especially important for interactive settings. Along with these two metrics, we also measure the average batch size during the inference process.
